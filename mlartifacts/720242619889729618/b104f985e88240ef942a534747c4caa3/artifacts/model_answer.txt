LoRA, which stands for Low-Rank Adaptation, refers to a parameter-efficient method of adapting pre-trained models like LMMs (Learning Models with Multimodal abilities) by updating only the bottom-ranked weight matrices. Specifically in this context:  

1. LoRA stands for Low Rank Adaptation and it is an approach used to fine-tune a pre-trained model such as LLaMAOneVision on specific tasks while preserving its general knowledge, often called "prior abilities." This technique adjusts the output distribution of the language representation layer or projection layers in question answering models without overhauling entire sections of parameters. Instead, LoRA uses low-rank matrices to capture task-specific adaptations and maintain a balance between retaining prior learned information (to mitigate catastrophic forgetting) and learning new skills effectively for sequential tasks within the curriculum.  

2. In our study presented in this document, we use LoRA as an example of selective tuning across various components like Vision Encoder + Projector+LLM (VEC-LLM), LLM alone or with additional projections and MLPs to mitigate forgetting while fine-tuning on the target tasks. The table in Section 6 shows that LoRA has a positive impact for learning but also causes some performance loss, hinting at potential downsides of selective tuning strategies like catastrophic interference or negative transfer when not used judiciously across different model components and task curriculum sequences.
